The catalog is organized into sections, the way the original was. An edition does not need to touch every section. Each item is tagged with one primary section.

| Section | Tag | What belongs here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understanding Minds | `minds` | Theories of intelligence, cognition, consciousness, philosophy of mind, functionalism, agency. |
| The Brain | `neuro` | Neuroscience, connectomics, neural recording, computational neuroscience, brain–computer interfaces. |
| Machine Learning | `ml` | Models, training, architectures, open weights, interpretability, evaluation. |
| Building with AI | `tools` | Libraries, frameworks, local inference, agents, developer tooling. |
| Silicon | `hardware` | Chip design, open PDKs, RISC-V, accelerators, neuromorphic hardware, FPGAs, dev boards. |
| Bodies and Sensors | `embodied` | Robotics, sensing, embodiment, the physical interface between minds and world. |
| Commons | `commons` | Open-source movements, licensing, datasets, governance, compute access, the economics of openness. |
| Learning | `learning` | Courses, textbooks, lectures, ways to teach yourself the material in this catalog. |
| Art and Visualization | `art` | Generative art, scientific imagery, diagrams, and the tools to make them. |

## Recurring formats

- **Catalog entry.** A short review, 80 to 250 words, ending in an Access line. The backbone of every edition.
- **Feature.** A longer essay or interview, 800 to 2,500 words.
- **Excerpt.** A passage from a book or paper with a short introduction.
- **Plate.** A full-width visual with a caption and credit.
- **Letters.** Reader responses and corrections.
