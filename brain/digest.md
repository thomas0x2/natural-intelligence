Every morning a scheduled Claude session reads this file, looks for new things that could go in the catalog, and emails a short list of candidates to the editor. It is a scouting report for the editor, not an edition. Nothing it finds goes into an edition without the editor reading it first.

Edit this file to change what the digest looks for or how it reads. The scheduled task reads it fresh from `main` on every run.

## Before searching

Read `vision.md`, `principles.md`, `topics.md`, `sources.md` and `backlog.md` in this folder, and skim the item names in `editions/`. Anything already listed in an edition or in the backlog's "Used" list is not new, so skip it.

Check the editor's inbox for the last seven days of digests (subject starts with "Natural Intelligence digest") when the email tool can read mail, and do not repeat an item that was already sent unless something important about it changed, such as a license change or a major release. Say so in the entry when that is the reason.

## What to look for

Look for things that appeared or changed meaningfully in roughly the last 48 hours (up to a week on quiet days):

- New or newly notable open-source projects: models, libraries, agent frameworks and harnesses, local-inference tools, interpretability tools, neuroscience software, chip design tools.
- Open-weights model releases, with their real license.
- Datasets, benchmarks and evaluation suites, especially ones that measure understanding rather than leaderboards.
- Papers and preprints that ship code or explain a mechanism well (arXiv cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.AR, q-bio.NC; bioRxiv neuroscience).
- Books, including new releases and free online editions.
- Long-form blog posts and essays with lasting value, not news takes.
- Open hardware: dev boards, RISC-V cores, open PDK news, Tiny Tapeout shuttles, neuromorphic chips, sensors.
- Courses, lecture series and textbooks.
- Art and visualization that helps people see minds, brains or machines.

Start with the watchlist in `sources.md`, then search the web broadly (GitHub trending, Hugging Face, Hacker News front page and "Show HN", lab and project blogs, arXiv listings). Open each candidate's primary source before listing it.

## How to judge

Apply the test in `vision.md`: would a capable, curious person be meaningfully more able to understand or build something after following this link?

- Prefer open source, open weights, open data and open hardware. A closed item makes the list only when it is genuinely important and has no open equivalent, and it is marked **Closed**.
- Check the license from the repository or model card, not from press coverage. Weights you can download but not build on are **weights-available**, not open.
- Prefer things that work at the scale of one person or a small lab.
- Skip pure news, funding announcements, product marketing, hype and doom.
- When something cannot be verified, either leave it out or say plainly what is unverified.

Aim for 5 to 12 candidates. On a thin day, send fewer and say it was a quiet day. Quality beats volume.

## Email format

- **To:** tbollenbach@icloud.com
- **Subject:** `Natural Intelligence digest · <Weekday, D Month YYYY> · <N> candidates`
- **Body:** HTML with a plain-text alternative. Keep styling minimal and inline so it reads well on a phone.

Open with one or two sentences on what stood out today. Then group candidates by catalog section (the tags in `topics.md`), strongest first. Each candidate has:

1. Name, linked to its primary source.
2. Kind (project, model, paper, book, post, tool, harness, benchmark, hardware, course, art) and section tag.
3. Two to three plain sentences in the catalog voice: what it is, why it fits, and its limits.
4. A draft Access line: link · license (or Closed) · cost · what you need to run or read it.
5. Confidence: high, medium or low that it belongs in the catalog, with a few words on why.

End with a short "Worth watching" list of one-line leads that are promising but not ready, and a footer line saying the digest is generated by a scheduled Claude task and that `brain/digest.md` controls it.

Use the catalog voice from `style.md`: plain, direct, no marketing language, no em-dashes.
