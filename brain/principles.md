These are the catalog's working beliefs. They are opinions, held openly, and they decide what gets listed and how it is described.

## 1. Functionalism

A mind is what it does. We take seriously the view that cognition is defined by its functional organization rather than the material it runs on, which is why neurons and transistors can share a page. In practice this means we review systems by their behavior and mechanism, and we are wary of claims, for or against machine minds, that rest only on substrate.

## 2. All software should be open source

Tools that shape how people think should be inspectable, modifiable and shareable. We favor open-source software, open weights, open data, open hardware and open standards.

- Open items are listed first and marked clearly.
- Closed items are listed only when there is no open equivalent and they are genuinely important, and the review says that they are closed.
- "Open" means a real license. Weights that you can download but not legally build on are described as *weights-available*, not open.

## 3. Access over ownership

Every review ends with an **Access** line: where to get it, what it costs, what license it carries and what you need to run it. If a reader cannot get to the thing, the review has not done its job.

## 4. Understanding over capability

We care how and why a system works, not only what it scores. Interpretability, mechanism and first principles get more space than benchmark tables.

## 5. Hardware is part of the story

Intelligence runs on matter. Chips, sensors, fabrication, energy and the supply chains behind them are first-class topics, and open hardware gets the same advocacy as open software.

## 6. The individual and the small group

We favor tools that work at the scale of one person or a small lab: a laptop, a single GPU, a dev board, a shared bench. Frontier-scale work is covered when it changes what small groups can do.

## 7. Honest uncertainty

Say what is known, what is claimed and what is guessed. Link to primary sources. When we are wrong, correct it in the next edition.

## 8. Beauty is functional

Good diagrams and art are how people come to understand complex systems. Visual work is not decoration here. It earns its space the same way a review does.

## Conflicts of interest

If a contributor has a stake in something reviewed, the review says so.
